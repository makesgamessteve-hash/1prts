# 04 · World, Scale & Theaters

## 1. Scale rules

- **1 world unit = 1 meter.** No exceptions.
- Humans are 1.6–1.9 m tall, Hesk 1.7–1.9 m, Ghorr 2.1–2.4 m, Durrow 1.3–1.5 m, Kobb 1.1 m,
  ridge giants 6 m.
- Doors are 1.0 × 2.2 m, castle gates 4 × 5 m, ship hatches 0.8 × 1.4 m.
- Storeys are 3.0–4.0 m; great halls 8–14 m; mine galleries 2.5 m; ship decks 1.9–2.2 m (sailors duck);
  Durrow tunnels 2.0 m (2.6 m on main roads, so carapace riders fit); deep caverns 20–150 m high.
- Ships, siege engines, beasts and flying creatures use believable sizes (see
  [05](05-units-structures-interiors.md)).
- **Distances are compressed, sizes and speeds are not.** Keeps sit closer together than in a
  real war so that a match fits into 45–90 minutes. A knight still gallops at about 40 km/h, and
  a galleon still sails at 15 km/h.

## 2. Theaters

A match is played over up to eight layers of one continuous world, from about 400 m below the
ground to 6 km above it.

| # | Theater | Space | Content | Home of |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High sky** | 3–6 km up, above the clouds | Floating citadels, sky galleons, elder drakes | Whoever wakes a citadel |
| 2 | **Storm layer** | 2–3 km, inside the cloud deck | Storms, lightning, near-zero visibility; fast winds that wind-barques ride and skyships hide in | Brine League (wind-barques) |
| 3 | **Sky** | Above the region, up to about 2 km | Balloons, wyverns, rocs, moths, albatrosses, skyships, drakes | Wildborn |
| 4 | **Field** | The map region (see sizes below) | Hills, forests, rivers, fords, villages, roads, mines, keeps, tunnel gates | Crown |
| 5 | **Water** | Rivers, lakes and the coast, on the surface | Harbors, fleets, landings, aether springs in the shallows | Brine League |
| 6 | **Under water** | Below the surface, down to about 60 m | Reefs, kelp forests, pearl beds, sunken wrecks; divers, mantas, diving bells, serpent riders | Brine League |
| 7 | **Tunnels** | 0–80 m below the field | Mine galleries, sapper tunnels, the Gloam tunnel network, tunnel wyrm burrows | Gloam Courts |
| 8 | **Deep caverns** | 80–400 m below the field | Fungus forests, underground lakes and rivers, glowstone grottos, aether seeps, the Gloam courts | Gloam Courts |

All eight layers are **one world**, so there is no loading between them. You can ride a lift cage
from a mine house down into the deep caverns, or dive from a ship's deck to a reef, without a
cut. Small maps may leave some layers out (see *Map sizes*).

- The **high sky** is cold, windy and hard to reach. Only skyships, rocs, drakes and wind-barques
  go that high, plus the lift-stones of a woken citadel.
- The **storm layer** is the cloud deck between sky and high sky. Inside it, sight drops to a
  few dozen meters, sending stones crackle, and lightning strikes anything tall. Fast winds carry
  wind-barques across the map. Anything flying to the high sky has to pass through it.
- **Under water**, sight is short and colors fade to blue-green with depth. Only Hesk, divers with
  breath charms, mantas, serpent riders and diving bells can stay there. Ships above see only
  shadows below.
- **Tunnels** run under the field. Everyone can dig short tunnels (sappers, mines), but only the
  Gloam dig long tunnel roads quickly. A tunnel is invisible from the surface until it opens,
  so sounds (echoes, tremors) are the only warning.
- The **deep caverns** have no day or night, only glowstone and torchlight. They are the Gloam's
  home and hold the richest glowstone and aether seeps. Others reach them through deep mine
  shafts (tier 4) or captured Gloam tunnels.

## 3. The high sky and the floating citadels

- Centuries ago the High Imperium ruled from **floating citadels** held up by great
  **aetherstones**. When the Imperium fell (the **Skyfall**), most citadels crashed. A few still
  **drift**, dark and silent, high above the land (see [08](08-setting-and-factions.md)).
- Each drifting citadel follows a **slow, predictable track** over the map. Its passes over
  key areas work like spy-satellite passes in a modern game: when a citadel passes overhead it
  can watch, drop troops or bombard; at other times it is out of reach.
- A side that **wakes** a citadel (by bringing aether and mages to its core) can **steer** it
  slowly and use its throne hall as a command post. Waking and holding a citadel is the late-game
  goal on large maps.
- Flying up is seamless. You stand on the skyship's deck as the ground falls away, pass through
  the storm layer and see the citadel's silhouette grow until you dock.

## 4. Moving interiors and reference frames

Big things move while people are inside them, so the world is built from **nested
reference frames**:

- Walking the deck of a galleon as it rolls in the swell, you move with the ship.
- Climbing the ladders inside a siege tower as it is pushed toward the wall, you stay inside it.
- Riding a lift cage down the mine shaft, you go down with the cage.
- In the gondola of a tunnel wyrm, you burrow with the beast and feel every turn.
- In a diving bell lowered from a ship, you sink with the bell and breathe its trapped air.
- On the Tidehold's quays and market, you drift with the whole raft-city.
- Walking among the huts on a behemoth's back, you sway with every step the beast takes.
- Standing in a roc's basket or a skyship's gondola, you fly with it.
- In a citadel's **aether core**, you float.
- **Things inside things**: a catapult is loaded onto a galleon, the galleon lands at a beach,
  and the commander was below decks the whole time.

How this works technically: [architecture/simulation.md](../architecture/simulation.md#3-reference-frames).

## 5. Environment

- **Day/night cycle**: one game day is 60 minutes by default (adjustable). It does not reach the
  deep caverns, where it is always dark, and it fades with depth under water. Nights need torches,
  lanterns and watch fires. Veyra has **two moons**, the pale Ilen and the red Corvane. A night
  with both full is almost as bright as dusk. A night with neither is truly dark.
- **Weather**: clear, overcast, rain, fog, storm, snow (depending on map). Weather affects sight,
  archery, sailing, flying and fire.
- **Wind**: direction and strength affect ships, skyships, arrows and how fire spreads.
- **Durrow and sunlight**: the Gloam see poorly in bright sun and well in the dark, so they fight
  best at night, underground and in fog.
- **Terrain** affects movement (roads, open ground, mud, forest, marsh), line of sight for eyes
  and sending stones, and cover.
- **Forests are a resource and a battlefield.** Trees are cut down for timber and do not grow
  back during a match. Forests block sight and slow horses. Forests can burn.

## 6. Destruction and fire

- **Buildings, ships and engines** have damage states: *intact → damaged* (smoke, flames,
  missing timbers, posts out of action) *→ ruined* (a wreck that blocks paths and can be
  salvaged for timber and gold).
- **Fire** is a real threat. Wooden buildings, ships, palisades and forests catch fire from fire
  arrows, firepots, dragon breath and spells. Fire spreads with the wind. Workers and crews put
  it out with buckets and sand. Stone does not burn, which is why stone keeps cost more.
- **Tunnels** can be collapsed on purpose (rockfall traps, Stonemeld, undercutters) or by heavy
  impacts, and low tunnels near rivers can flood.
- **Interiors** take damage **room by room**: fire, flooding (ships), collapse, smoke. Crews
  respond (see [05](05-units-structures-interiors.md)).
- **Terrain** gets scorch marks and craters. Deformable voxel terrain (dug trenches, tunnels dug
  at run time, collapsed galleries) is an optional later feature (see [09](09-art-and-audio-direction.md)).
