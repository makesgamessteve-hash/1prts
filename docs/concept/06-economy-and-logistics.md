# 06 · Economy & Logistics

The economy is classic fantasy RTS (workers gather gold and timber, food limits your army) and
it is physical. Every sack, log, block and pearl is carried by someone you can see, along a path
that can be raided.

## 1. Resources

There are **ten resources**, but no faction uses more than **six**. Five are shared by everyone
(or nearly everyone), and each faction adds one or two of its own. This keeps each faction as
easy to read as a classic RTS while making the four feel different.

| Resource | Kind | Source | Carried by | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gold** | Shared | Gold mines (they run out) | Workers, in sacks | Almost everything |
| **Timber** | Shared (not the Gloam) | Trees (forests shrink as they are cut) | Workers, as logs | Buildings, ships, engines, arrows |
| **Aether** | Shared, rare | Wells and shrines on land; springs in the shallows; seeps in the deep caverns | Wagons, skimmers, rafts, lighters, delvers | Magic, skyships, drakes, signature units, waking citadels |
| **Food** | Shared, capacity | Farmsteads, herd pens, mycel gardens, fisheries, captured villages | — (not stored) | Sets how many **folk** you can support |
| **Folk** | Shared | Villages and housing, within the limit food sets | On foot | Crewing every unit, building and ship |
| **Powder** | Crown only, crafted | Made at the Dwarven Forgeworks from gold and timber | Supply wagons | Bombards, bombard towers, galleon guns, sappers' kegs |
| **Bone** | Wildborn only | Herd pens (slowly), plus beasts killed and wrecks picked over by Kobb | Kobb, pack beasts | Bone forge upgrades, rams, bonehulks, totems |
| **Stone** | Gloam only | Cut while digging tunnels, and at quarry faces | Delvers, on sledges | Every Gloam building, basalt guard armor, tunnel gates |
| **Glowstone** | Gloam only | Crystal veins in the deep caverns and glowstone grottos | Delvers | Light underground, crystal magic, lumen weapons, moth lamps |
| **Pearls** | Brine League only | Pearl beds and reefs under the water | Pearl divers, pearl lighters | Tide and storm magic, the Tidehold, trade at market towns |

### Which faction uses what

| Faction | Its six resources |
|---|---|
| Crown of Esterholt | Gold, timber, aether, food, folk, **powder** |
| Wildborn Clans | Gold, timber, aether, food, folk, **bone** |
| Gloam Courts | Gold, aether, food, folk, **stone**, **glowstone** (no timber: they build in rock) |
| Brine League | Gold, timber, aether, food, folk, **pearls** |

Faction resources cannot be gathered by other factions. Captured stocks can be sold at a
**market town** for gold (see §3).

## 2. Folk and food: the army's limit

- **Food** works as a cap. Each farmstead, herd pen, mycel garden or fishery feeds a set number of
  folk. Lose one and you can't train more units until you rebuild it. Units you already have
  still fight.
- **Folk** are real people. They join from villages you control (a slow, steady trickle) and
  from your food buildings. Training turns them into soldiers, sailors, mages, riders and divers.
- Every unit needs a full crew before it is finished: 1 for a serf, 10 for a trebuchet, 250 for
  a war galleon, 140 for a behemoth, 600 for a Tidehold. Losing a big hull costs folk as well as
  gold and timber.
- Survivors gain experience. Rescuing a downed wyvern rider or carrying the wounded back to the
  infirmary is worthwhile.

## 3. Gathering and delivery

- **Gold**: workers walk into the mine (you can follow them down), come back up with a sack and
  carry it to the nearest delivery point. Mines slowly run out, so you have to expand.
- **Timber**: workers fell trees one by one. Forests shrink over a match, which opens new paths
  and new lines of sight. A sawmill, timber camp or timber yard near the forest shortens the walk.
- **Stone**: every tunnel the Gloam dig produces stone, so digging and building feed each other.
  The stonecutters' hall is the delivery point.
- **Glowstone**: veins in the deep caverns, richest in glowstone grottos. Taking a grotto means
  holding a place far below the enemy's reach, or far below your own.
- **Pearls**: pearl divers swim down to reef beds and come back up with nets. Pearl lighters work
  deeper beds from the surface. Both are easy prey for serpent riders and hull-borers.
- **Bone**: herd pens give a little bone over time. Kobb gather far more from the field after a
  battle and from wrecks, which gives the Wildborn a reason to fight.
- **Powder**: made, not gathered. The Forgeworks turns gold and timber into powder over time.
  The powder store is a fire risk.
- **Aether**: wells and shrines are slow but steady. Springs in the shallows and seeps in the deep
  caverns are rich but exposed.
- **Delivery points** decide your gathering speed. Building them close to the resource is the
  core economic skill, as in classic RTS.
- **Market towns** (neutral) buy and sell any resource for gold at prices that change with
  supply and demand. The Brine League gets better prices.
- **Field supplies** (optional match rule): armies far from a delivery point use up arrows,
  powder and food. Supply wagons, pack beasts and pack beetles carry more. A company without
  supplies loses morale and effectiveness.

## 4. Construction

1. **Choose** a building on the field map, the quartermaster's ledger or the war table.
2. **Place** it with the marking staff's outline at a site you can see, or at the war table on
   land that has been scouted. The outline checks slope, space and where it is allowed (a harbor
   needs a shore, a mine house needs a gold mine, a Gloam hall needs rock below it, a tide temple
   needs water).
3. **Workers** are sent and bring the materials.
4. **Building stages**: staked out → foundations → frame and scaffolding → walls and roof →
   finished. Gloam halls are dug out instead: marked → cut → shaped → carved → finished. You can
   walk in from the third stage onward.
5. **Presence** speeds up building by 25% (see [02](02-the-commander.md)).

Wooden buildings are cheap and burn. Stone buildings (keeps, castle walls, towers, Gloam halls)
cost more and take longer, and they do not burn. Gloam halls can only be attacked from their
gates or by tunnelling into them.

## 5. Tiers and upgrades

There are **six tiers**. The first three come from **upgrading your headquarters**, the way
classic fantasy RTS does it. The last three are **mandates**: costly decrees (bought with gold and
aether at your top headquarters) that each need certain buildings first.

| Tier | Name | How it is reached | Opens (all factions, in their own versions) |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Founding | First headquarters stage | Workers, basic foot, ranged, scouts, food buildings, barracks, delivery points, watchtowers |
| T2 | Muster | Second headquarters stage | Cavalry and fast beasts, pikes, tower upgrades, smithy-type upgrades, transport boats, short sapper tunnels |
| T3 | Siegecraft | Third headquarters stage | Siege engines, warships, first casters, walls and gates |
| T4 | Mastery | Mandate; needs a caster building and a smithy-type upgrade | Second casters, advanced upgrades (Lantern Knights), light flyers, deep caverns opened through mine shafts |
| T5 | Dominion | Mandate; needs a harbor or roost and two aether sources | Heavy warships, big flyers, signature units (behemoth, tunnel wyrm, Tidehold, dwarven skyship) |
| T6 | Ascendancy | Mandate; needs T5 and a far-sight building | Sky galleons, elder drakes, wind-barques in the high sky, waking a floating citadel |

| Faction | Headquarters stages (T1 → T2 → T3) | Mandates (T4–T6) are called |
|---|---|---|
| Crown | Holdfast → Keep → High Castle | Royal Charters |
| Wildborn | Longhouse → War Lodge → Antler Throne | Great Rites |
| Gloam | Delve → Hollow Court → Sunken Throne | Deep Oaths |
| Brine | Wharf hall → Guildhall → Admiralty | League Articles |

**Upgrades** are bought at workshops: better blades and armor, fire bolts, longer-range sending
stones, faster workers, stronger walls, deeper tunnels, better diving gear. All of this is
defined in Lua, so mods can build any tech tree they want.

## 6. Economic pacing (1v1, M map, reference values)

| Time | Typical state |
|---|---|
| 0–5 min | T1: headquarters, first mine and timber (or stone) site, two food buildings, scouts |
| 5–15 min | T2: barracks, more workers, first company of soldiers, a forward watchtower, cavalry |
| 15–30 min | T3: first siege engine, harbor or lake dock, relay towers, first casters |
| 30–45 min | T4: second casters, light flyers, the first push into the deep caverns or under the water |
| 45–60 min | T5: signature units, big hulls, raids on enemy gatherers and pearl beds |
| 60+ min | T6: sky galleons or drakes, the race to wake a floating citadel, deciding assaults |
