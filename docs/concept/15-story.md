# 15 · Story

The story of *Banner & Bastion* starts at one castle and grows into the war for the sky. It is
set in Veyra six centuries after the Skyfall ([08](08-setting-and-factions.md)), and like
everything else in the game it is told only through the commander's eyes and ears.

## 1. Premise

You are the newly sworn **Marshal of Harrowmark**, Lord or Lady Marshal of the Crown of
Esterholt. You were sworn in at dawn in Esterholt and sent north the same day, because the
Marshal before you has disappeared.

**Harrowmark** is the Crown's great border castle on the northern march, where the chalk downs
meet the dark pines of the Ashpine forest, a day's ride south of the **Skyfall Meres**: crater
lakes ringed with the ruins of fallen citadels. It is also the Crown's **proving fortress**.
Every new Marshal is proven here, at the oldest war table in the kingdom, before the Crown gives
them an army. (The Proving Ground map of milestone M1 is Harrowmark.)

## 2. The hook

- **The north has gone silent.** Nine days ago the sending stones of the **Merewatch**, the
  chain of watchtowers on the rim of the meres, stopped speaking. Their last report: *"Smoke on
  the Caldmere. The stone grows warm."* Since then the stones only hiss.
- **The Marshal is missing.** Lord Marshal **Corwin Ashby** rode north with the *Second Lance*
  six days ago to find out why. He has not sent a word, and he has not sounded a horn.
- **The Wildborn are gathering.** Ghorr hunters watch Harrowmark's walls from the ridge road,
  night after night, without raiding. Deep in the Ashpine the clans are coming together, and
  their shamans' spirit-fire has turned white.
- **What is really happening** (revealed over the campaign): under the largest crater lake, the
  **Caldmere**, lies **Caldrith**, a citadel of the High Imperium that fell in the Skyfall. Its
  aetherstone is waking. Its hum drowns out every sending stone nearby (so the Merewatch went
  silent), it warms the lake until it steams (the "smoke"), and every people of Veyra has felt
  it. Ashby went down to the Caldmere to see it for himself.

## 5. The campaign: *The Skyfall Wars*

A prologue and eight acts. Each act is built on what one roadmap milestone
([12](12-roadmap.md)) adds, so an act's first mission can be prototyped as a scenario as soon as
its milestone lands; the full campaign ships with Release 1.0 (M10). Played as the Crown, the
acts below are the main line; the other sides have their own missions on the same events (§6).

| Act | Milestone | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Prologue: Harrowmark** | M1 Proving Ground | You arrive on the road before the King's Gate. Walk through the Outer Ward and the Lion Gate, meet the household and the garrison, climb the Raven Tower and the Great Keep to the war room. The stones of the north stay silent. *(Playable now.)* |
| **I · The Silent Watch** | M2 First Command | Your first commands from Harrowmark's war table: serfs, gold and timber, a beacon spire to reach past the silence, a company of men-at-arms. Wildborn raiders strike the mill at the ford. The castellan's proving is the Training mission. |
| **II · The Border Marches** | M3 Ride Out | You ride north with the Lantern Knights. The first Merewatch tower is empty, its stone cracked from inside. A siege tower against a tower the Wildborn hold; you find Tam Durran alive, and learn that Ashby went down to the Caldmere. |
| **III · Timber and Gold** | M4 Hold and Harvest | The clans gather under a behemoth and Huntlord Urgra besieges Harrowmark through the winter. Hold the castle, feed it, fight its fires; if you fall, your banner passes to your second. |
| **IV · The Saltcoast** | M5 Sail and Wing | To break the siege the Crown comes up the river by ship and over the Ashpine by skyship: the *Harbor Siege*. |
| **V · Above the Clouds** | M6 Skyfall | Caldrith rises out of the steaming Caldmere. Ashby is aboard: he went down to wake it, not to fight it. *Storm the Citadel*. |
| **VI · The Hollow Roads** | M7 Hollow Roads | The Gloam Courts open their sealed gates for the first time since the Skyfall: Caldrith's broken chains run down into the deep caverns. *Under the Hill*. |
| **VII · Deep Water** | M8 Deep Water | The Brine League arrives with the storms the citadel stirs up; whoever holds a citadel holds the sky trade. The *Reef War*. |
| **VIII · The Custodians Wake** | M10 Release 1.0 | Waking Caldrith has woken its guardians, and every drifting citadel with them. The four peoples must choose: steer the citadels, share them or sink them. |

M9 (Every Screen) is a polish milestone and has no act of its own.

## 6. The same war from the other sides

- **The Wildborn Clans.** The clans remember the High Imperium as conquerors, and the Crown
  claims to be its heir. When the shamans' spirit-fire burned white over the Caldmere, Huntlord
  Urgra read it plainly: the sky-tyrants are waking, and the Crown's new Marshal has come to
  raise them. The clans gather to burn Harrowmark before it can. To them the siege of Act III
  is the war's just cause, and the Marshal is the aggressor.
- **The Gloam Courts.** The Durrow felt the aetherstone's hum through the rock long before the
  surface did: crystal sang in the deep, and old tunnels shook. The elders of the Hollow Court
  sealed their gates at the Skyfall and swore never to let a citadel rise again. They want
  Caldrith buried, or its aetherstone for their own crystal magic; their councils cannot agree
  which.
- **The Brine League.** At sea the war looks like weather: storms out of a clear sky and tides
  that run wrong, which the Hesk feel before anyone. The League's captains see profit. They sell
  powder and passage to every side, and the Tidehold council wants a citadel of its own,
  because whoever holds the sky holds the trade.
- **The Custodians** have no side. They wake with the citadels and "repair" the land the way the
  Imperium wanted it.

## 7. How the story is told

Story follows the First-Person Contract ([01](01-vision-and-pillars.md#the-first-person-contract)).
Nothing is shown from outside the commander's eyes.

- **Voices you hear.** People speak to you where they stand, and their words appear as
  subtitles with their name (the minimal HUD). Walk up to someone and use them to talk; they say
  their lines in turn, and introduce themselves with their title when they start from the top.
- **The sending stone.** Distant voices come through the stone on your belt (captains,
  watchtowers, the gate), labelled as such, crackling when the link is weak and silent when it
  is cut. Sounds without a speaker are captioned in brackets.
- **Things you read.** Notice boards, letters, dispatches, ledgers and maps are objects in the
  world, drawn on in-world surfaces. You walk up to them and read them.
- **The war table's illusions.** The scryer can show you far places, past battles or what a
  scout saw as illusions on the table: a display you look at, not a cut-away.
- **No cutscenes from outside.** No cameras, no narrator over black, no cinematic cut-aways.
  Even the prologue is spoken by people the Marshal can hear. If a scene must be watched, the
  commander watches it from where they stand.
- **Deterministic and skippable.** Story lines are timed in simulation ticks, so a recorded
  game replays them exactly. The prologue plays only when a map is started from the front-end
  menu and can be turned off (Options, Game); test and `--start` runs never play it.

plain ASCII, because the UI font has no other glyphs.
